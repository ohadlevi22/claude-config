# Claymation & Plasticine

## Plain English
Transform photos or scenes into handcrafted clay sculptures and stop-motion animation style. The subject looks like it was physically sculpted from modeling clay, complete with fingerprint marks, tool impressions, and the charming imperfections of real handmade art. Ranges from cute viral clay portraits to dark gothic puppet animation.

## When to Use
- Converting photos into clay/plasticine characters
- Stop-motion animation style scenes
- Handcrafted figurine portraits
- Clay world dioramas
- Cute collectible-style character renders
- Dark/gothic puppet animation style

## Key Ingredients

### The Secret: Imperfection Language (CRITICAL)

The single biggest differentiator between convincing and unconvincing clay prompts is specifying **imperfections as evidence of physical process**, not just "texture":

**DO** (specific, process-based):
- "visible fingerprint depressions where sculptor pressed plasticine"
- "subtle thumbwork marks around facial features"
- "gentle compression marks at joints"
- "organic irregularities in cheek contours"
- "slightly uneven edges from hand-sculpting"
- "tool marks from sculpting implements"

**DON'T** (vague, surface-level):
- "textured surface" (too generic)
- "rough texture" (produces digital noise)
- "clay-like" (ambiguous)

### Camera
- **Lens**: Medium (50mm equivalent) for portraits, macro for detail shots
- **Angle**: Slightly low angle emphasizes miniature set scale
- **Framing**: Medium shot for full character; eye-level for immersion
- **Depth**: Shallow DOF typical of macro photography (sells miniature scale)
- **Key phrase**: "eye-level camera position for miniature subject, shallow depth of field typical of macro photography"

### Lighting
- **Style**: Soft diffused studio lighting at TABLETOP scale
- **Direction**: Overhead softbox with warm tungsten fill
- **Quality**: Soft shadows that reveal 3D clay depth without harsh contrast
- **Effects**: Subtle rim light for edge separation
- **CRITICAL RULE**: Avoid grand lighting terms ("vast skylight", "distant sun") -- they break miniature scale. Use "contained sources" and "warm practical lights"
- **Key phrase**: "soft diffused lighting creating gentle shadows on plasticine surface, warm color temperature typical of tungsten studio bulbs"

### Texture Keywords (ranked by effectiveness)
1. `visible fingerprint impressions` - the #1 most important clay cue
2. `hand-sculpted imperfections` - sells the handmade feel
3. `subtle surface irregularities` - organic, not digital
4. `matte clay finish` - prevents glossy/digital drift
5. `thumbwork marks` - specific sculpting process evidence
6. `gentle compression marks` - at joints and edges
7. `slightly uneven color transitions` - paint/pigment variation
8. `plasticine ridges` - surface detail

### Material Terms (each produces slightly different results)
| Term | Result |
|------|--------|
| `claymation` | Classic stop-motion, Aardman-leaning |
| `plasticine` | Softer, more malleable, visible finger marks |
| `clay render` | Cleaner, more 3D/digital, semi-realistic |
| `polymer clay` | Finer detail, collectible figurine quality |
| `sculpted clay` | Emphasis on dimensional form |
| `modeling clay` | Simpler, more childlike |
| `handmade clay figure` | Warm, imperfect, craft-oriented |

## Pro Tips

1. **Imperfection is everything** -- "fingerprint depressions where sculptor pressed plasticine" beats "textured surface" every time
2. **Scale your lighting** -- always think "tabletop studio", never "cinematic landscape"
3. **Material term matters** -- `plasticine` = soft/malleable, `polymer clay` = fine detail, `claymation` = classic Aardman
4. **Place clay terms early** in the prompt for strongest effect (especially in Midjourney)
5. **For photo conversions**, front-load "maintain facial features and likeness"
6. **Matte finish** is essential -- without it, AI defaults to glossy/digital
7. **Stop-motion cues** even in stills: "frame-by-frame captured moment", "hand-adjusted armature"
8. **Sub-style anchors**: Name a studio or film for instant style lock (Aardman, Laika, Wes Anderson)
9. **Don't over-texture** -- specify WHERE imperfections appear ("around joints and facial features") to avoid noisy output
